The team

Select an agent. A person on the People Ops team stays in the middle of every one.

How we communicate with them

Three ways in, and the same rule for all: a person reviews before anything reaches an employee or changes a record.

Ask in chat

Ask in Slack and get a drafted answer, checklist, or summary back in the thread.

From the action form

A submitted request starts the matching agent's checklist and reminders.

On a schedule

Deadline alerts, weekly digests, and month-end checklists arrive without asking.

Where the agents work, and how it all connects

No new app for anyone to learn. The agents show up in Slack, Notion and Google Workspace, and Zapier and n8n carry the data between them. They only touch what a person has shared.

1Ask in SlackSomeone posts in the People channel or uses a request form.
2Agent draftsIt reads the approved page or template and writes a draft in the thread.
3Saved in Notion or DocsDrafts, checklists and summaries land in the wiki or a shared doc.
4A person approvesNothing reaches an employee or changes a record before this step.
5Record updatesThe HRIS stays the system of record. The agent only prepares the change.

Slack

Where people ask and get answers
  • AskPeople answers routine questions in a public People channel, with a clear route to a person.
  • Private, restricted channels for sensitive reports, immigration and leave. No personal details in open channels.
  • Reminders for open enrollment, I-9 signatures, expiry dates and cycle deadlines.
  • Weekly digest to managers: what is done, what is open.

Notion

Where the written record lives
  • The agents work from the handbook, HR wiki and policy pages. When a policy is missing, they draft one for a person to edit and approve.
  • Onboarding and offboarding checklists and the first-90-days plan live as templates.
  • Investigation and escalation process pages, with a written reason for each decision.
  • Each agent has a one-page BRD and a runbook, with a named owner and review date.

Google Workspace

Docs, Sheets, Drive, Calendar, Gmail
  • Docs: policies, notices and company-wide comms drafted for review.
  • Sheets: the immigration tracker, the open enrollment checklist and cycle trackers, shared by permission.
  • Calendar: renewal dates, cycle milestones and cohort start dates.
  • Drive and Gmail: drafts only. Agents never send, and sensitive files stay in restricted folders.

HRIS (Rippling)

The system of record
  • Hire, change and exit events trigger the matching checklist.
  • Agents read counts and report data-quality gaps. They do not write to employee records.
  • A person makes every record change, so the audit trail stays clean.

Zapier

The quick no-code connector
  • A hire, change or exit in the HRIS starts a Zap that posts the request in Slack and opens the matching Notion checklist.
  • The Zap hands the agent only the counts and template it needs. No names, pay or documents pass through.
  • Approval buttons in Slack release the next step. Until a person clicks, nothing is created or sent.
  • Each run adds a row to a Google Sheet log: request, output and reviewer.

n8n

For longer, branching workflows
  • Runs the multi-step jobs: deadline scans, weekly digests and renewal alerts that branch by country or status.
  • Calls the agent for a draft at the right step, then pauses for a person to approve.
  • Reads Sheets and Notion pages, and posts results back to Slack on a schedule.
  • Failures go to a People Ops channel, so a missed run is noticed the same day.

Guardrails

What keeps the agents safe to use in an HR team.

Non-PII first

No names, ID numbers, pay, medical, or immigration documents in prompts or chat.

Systems of record stay put

The HRIS, payroll, and benefits platforms hold the data. Agents work from templates and counts.

A person approves

Anything that changes a record or reaches an employee is reviewed first.

Logged and reviewed

Request, output, and reviewer are recorded. Legal templates get counsel review.

Before any agent goes live: a BRD

Each agent starts as a one-page business requirements document. Select a step to see what happens, who is involved, and an example.

BBusinessRRequirementsDDocument

New to the term? A BRD is a short, plain-language page written before anything gets built. It spells out what we are trying to fix, what the tool should and should not do, and how we will know it worked. Think of it as a recipe card or a creative brief: everyone agrees on the dish before anyone starts cooking.

📝 What it isOne page that Legal, Security, Finance and People Ops can all read and agree on.
🙅 What it is notA technical spec or a long contract. No jargon needed to read it.
⏱️ When it happensBefore the build, so surprises show up early and cheaply.
